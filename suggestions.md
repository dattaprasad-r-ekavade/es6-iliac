# Suggestions: Ratna Bay story campaign

**Created:** 8 September 2026  
**Status:** creative proposal for review, not established canon or implemented gameplay.  
**Target:** a satisfying first campaign of 8–10 hours, followed by optional continued play.

This document develops the existing material in `Docs/SCRIPT.md`, `Docs/STORY.md`, `Docs/STORY_AND_SYSTEMS.md`, and the fort fragments. It preserves Uttara, the fifty-seven, the order's name, the shortage, the household register, and a single ending. New characters, dialogue, supernatural rules, mission structures, and consequences below are suggestions. Existing source files and story documents are not changed by this proposal.

## 1. The story worth building

You join an order that promises somebody will come for you. At first that promise means a recovered pack and a lamp with your name beneath it. By the end, the order must decide whether it means anything when keeping it stops being profitable.

The campaign begins with useful work, familiar faces, and small improvements. It gradually exposes a system that counts output more carefully than people. The final act asks the order to become the rescue service its name always implied: reach Uttara, relieve her, and take collective responsibility for the threshold she has guarded alone.

**Recommended creative change:** the ending should replace solitary sacrifice with shared responsibility. The player should not simply become the next abandoned person behind the door. Uttara's rescue of fifty-seven proves that saving people is possible; the order's last act should learn from that success.

The province still pays a real price for closing its oldest deep seam. Hospitals need supplies, jobs disappear, and the army demands explanations. Preparation changes particular lives. It does not make the decision painless.

### Assessment of the existing material

| Material | Current creative assessment | Priority |

These scores assess concepts, not implementation or guaranteed player reception. The route toward a stronger story is causality, relationships, and visible consequences—not more exposition.

## 2. Canon to retain and decisions to settle

Retain the short chronology: Uttara disappears in 310 BCE; the sealed entrance is forced in 305; the campaign is in 300. She has been below for ten years, and the entrance remained sealed for five. The order is approximately one year old. Refer to the administrators who later founded it when discussing the older breach, never to the order as an organisation that already existed.

Bhagiratha is the missing husband's name. The miners named the order. Honouring Uttara and naming the order after her are different claims; preserve the former without accidentally writing the latter.

Before implementation, reconcile the older multiple-ending tables with the single-ending direction. Adopt one campaign duration, and define whether a depth milestone means a room entered or cleared. Do not rely on a document's statement that content is reconciled without checking the actual fragment.

### Proposed supernatural rules

1. **Prana restrains manifestations; using it does not create an individual's guilt.** Extraction, storage, and consumption disturb an already damaged seam in different ways. Provincial activity continues regardless of the player's weapon choice.
2. **A true record can settle a particular haunting.** It requires a name, corroboration, and acknowledgment by the living. An arbitrary name typed into a ledger cannot release a spirit. Some dead cannot yet be identified.
3. **Settling one haunting does not repair the deep breach.** The seam is a physical source of uncontrolled prana discharge. Personal restitution and containment solve different problems.
4. **Uttara guards the breached entrance; she is not a magical battery.** Her repeated defence has limited the danger since the seal was broken, but cannot rebuild the seal alone.
5. **The final repair needs work on both sides, then a withdrawal route.** Earlier survey notes establish how the order can complete it without abandoning a replacement. A maintained watch and the end of forced extraction keep it closed; no unbreakable spell solves politics forever.

These are proposed clarifications, especially rules 3–5. Demonstrate them through an early haunting, damaged workings, and survey evidence before asking the player to accept them at the ending.

## 3. Pacing an 8–10-hour first campaign

Treat elapsed time as a playtest target, not a gate. Skilled players may finish faster; players who die often should still receive new material.

An initial budget is **45–55 ordinary descents**, averaging roughly seven minutes including their usual return visit: about 5.25–6.4 hours. Add **1.75–2.25 hours** for the house, seam, fort defence, and major scenes, plus **0.5–1 hour** for learning and exploration beyond ordinary visits. That gives a provisional range of roughly **7.5–9.7 hours**. These are estimates to replace with measurements. Do not also add all ordinary hub time a second time.

| Approximate time | Act | Change the player should feel |
|---|---|---|
| 0–1.5 hours | A place worth returning to | Learn the job and become known to three people |
| 1.5–3.5 hours | The missing column | Learn that the dead and the books disagree |
| 3.5–5.5 hours | The house and the audit | Obtain proof and cause an institutional response |
| 5.5–7.5 hours | The order answers | Prepare people, uncover the breach order, reach Uttara |
| 7.5–9.5 hours | The relief | Defend the fort, repair the seam, see the consequences |

Plan 12 major beats and an initial 45–60 short reactions. Add more only where playtesting finds empty stretches or repeated lines. Offer something personally relevant on most returns: a reaction, changed object, practical benefit, or new objective. Do not force a conversation every time.

## 4. Proposed campaign beats

Every beat below needs a remembered result and a clear reason the next becomes available. Rank can establish access, but major revelations should also follow earlier discoveries.

### Beat 1 — An empty line

**Placement:** first 10–15 minutes, before or after the first descent.

Ganaka asks the recruit's name, then asks who should be told if they do not return. Revati checks the lamp's fastening. Ketu, one of Uttara's fifty-seven, repairs the gate while arguing about the price of rope.

**Player action:** choose a short reason for joining: a debt, someone at home, or steady work. This is a dialogue preference, not a class or stat bonus.

**Sample:**

> Ganaka: “Your name first. The debt goes in another book.”
>
> Revati: “Bring the lamp back. If you cannot, somebody will.”

**Result:** the name appears on an actual line in the hall. A later successor receives their own line. The opening establishes procedure and kindness before accusation.

### Beat 2 — A repair that holds

**Placement:** first hour, after the first completed run; use a supportive variation after early deaths.

The returned stones let the order replace a failing lamp or mend a bench. Ketu mentions that Uttara checked every knot twice. He complains about it before admitting that he survived because she did.

**Player action:** inspect the repair or ask one practical question. No errand is required.

**Result:** the hub visibly improves. Preserve this object for the fort crisis. The player should remember that the economy also buys real good.

### Beat 3 — Twelve in the book

**Placement:** about hour 1–1.5; trigger after an early room clear.

A defeated preta leaves a broken tally and a short testimony. The room is safe before its text appears. Ganaka's matching roll has fewer names.

**Sample:**

> “Nalin. East gallery. Twelve on your paper. Thirteen at the rope.”

**Player action:** bring the automatically recorded discovery to Ganaka. Ketu recognises the work mark and corroborates the account.

**Result:** Ganaka adds one name. On a later visit, the associated haunting is absent from a small authored encounter or identifiable room event. This demonstrates a rule rather than promising that every ledger correction clears a mine.

### Beat 4 — Who gets the next stone?

**Placement:** hours 1.5–2.5.

Visakha has enough supply for tonight, not tomorrow. Mara can provide a reserve if the order commits a share of its next payment. The player can back that commitment or pledge personal stock.

**Player action:** choose the source of a modest reserve. Both options are viable and clearly priced; neither requires permanent loss of essential gear.

**Sample:**

> Visakha: “Do not promise me a season. Promise me tomorrow, and mean it.”

**Result:** the clinic has a visible reserve, or must move non-urgent treatment to daylight. Later dialogue remembers the promise. The choice introduces allocation without a hidden morality score or a surprise patient death.

### Beat 5 — The cart goes past

**Placement:** hours 2.5–3.5.

Lohasena's request is refused while an army delivery passes through the yard. Bhadrasena can release repair materials within his authority, but needs the order to accept responsibility in writing.

**Player action:** sign for a repair allocation, or use an available salvage alternative. This changes equipment preparation for a later defence, not basic access to combat.

**Result:** a repaired gate brace or protected clinic entrance becomes visible. The captain is constrained but capable of useful action. The player learns that institutional rules can be worked with.

### Beat 6 — The warm house

**Placement:** hours 3.5–4.5; authored midpoint mission, approximately 20–30 minutes.

Vasudatta welcomes the order into a house that is warm, well lit, and almost insultingly ordinary. The job is to clear the cellar. An enormous reserve glows behind a screen while upstairs the host complains about prices.

**Player action:** clear finite rooms, discover the household register, and hear the testimony of a servant omitted from the town roll. The critical register is on the required route; optional corroboration rewards exploration.

Loose stones are available. Their destination matters: the player can allocate recovered stock to the clinic, the order's preparations, or personal use. Show the ownership dispute plainly rather than declaring every pickup wicked.

**Sample:**

> Vasudatta: “The lamps? Those are an expense. The reserve is an asset.”
>
> A servant's record: “Soma. Thirty-one years in this house. No household of her own.”

**Result:** the player brings back physical proof. The shortage is partly falling output, partly priority allocation, and heavily worsened by hoarding. Avoid a speech reducing it to one cause.

### Beat 7 — A copy outside the room

**Placement:** hours 4.5–5.

Chandrashri compares the household register with official totals. For the first time she can send more than a claim that the numbers are wrong.

**Player action:** authorise a witnessed copy for the inspector. An optional second copy can be lodged with Ganaka. The player chooses how to protect the evidence, not whether the entire campaign may proceed.

**Sample:**

> Chandrashri: “They have returned my arithmetic three times. This time I am sending names.”

**Result:** the audit becomes unavoidable. This is a consequence of the player's investigation, not an announcement on a run counter.

### Beat 8 — A narrow mandate

**Placement:** hours 5–5.75.

The inspector arrives. He refuses a demand to abolish the quota, but cannot dismiss a witnessed discrepancy under the procedure he enforces.

**Player action:** present the record and witness. Optional corroboration secures a temporary hold on a disputed shipment, producing a preparation benefit. Essential progress cannot fail because the player misses one dialogue keyword.

**Sample:**

> Inspector: “I cannot reduce an order from the capital. I can refuse to certify a false return.”

**Result:** an archive is opened for audit, exposing the older order to breach Uttara's seal. The antagonist's limited concession makes him credible. He remains responsible for what he declines to examine.

### Beat 9 — We signed it

**Placement:** hours 5.75–6.5.

The player confronts Vasumitra with the breach order. He admits that the administrators authorised the opening five years before founding the Bhagiratha. Revati learns enough to challenge the order's account of itself.

**Player action:** ask what is behind the seal and insist on a survey rather than another quota expedition.

**Sample:**

> Vasumitra: “We did not lose the door. We ordered it opened.”
>
> Revati: “Then this time we write down who goes through it.”

**Result:** the finite seam mission opens. Its purpose is reconnaissance and contact, not killing a named boss. Revati begins to say “we” when planning with the player.

### Beat 10 — Still at her post

**Placement:** hours 6.5–7.5; first part of the authored seam mission.

The party finds repaired supports, old rescue markings, and evidence of years of repeated defence. Uttara attacks threats crossing the threshold; she does not pursue the player as prey.

**Player action:** survive an encounter, restore a ward mechanism, and use the recovered tally to establish recognition during a safe interval. Simply reducing her health to zero is not the completion condition.

**Sample:**

> Uttara: “How many came with you?”
>
> Player: “All of them are still here.”

**Result:** discover that rebuilding the seal requires support from both sides. The survey establishes a side passage and a coordinated withdrawal, avoiding a last-minute escape device. The party returns to prepare relief; Uttara's position remains held.

### Beat 11 — The lamps go out

**Placement:** roughly hour 7.5–8.5; one full fort crisis.

The existing breach worsens during an already foreshadowed discharge. The survey brought warning; it did not arbitrarily punish the player for learning the truth. Earlier local disturbances now become a full assault.

**Player action:** clear familiar rooms, restore routes, and organise the order. The repaired gate and clinic reserve affect difficulty and aftermath. The evidence copy gives a redundant way to preserve the record.

Use short combat phases and safe regrouping intervals. Pause any rescue timer during essential dialogue. Do not make reading speed decide an NPC's fate. Initially prefer recoverable injuries and relocation over permanent deaths; only add lethal branches if they justify their writing and testing cost.

**Result:** the fort survives, imperfectly. Everyone gathers in one room. The political arguments happen among people the player has just protected.

### Beat 12 — The relief

**Placement:** final 30–45 minutes including epilogue; reuse the established seam route and change its encounter state.

Chandrashri argues for the public record. Vasumitra argues for a controlled supply. Visakha demands a transition that does not abandon patients. Revati insists that nobody remain alone below.

The campaign has one resolution: repair and close the deep seam. The player's agency lies in the preparations and commitments that make it possible, not a hidden score selecting a different film.

**Player action:** escort the repair effort through a short final encounter, hold a known position while the seal is rebuilt, signal the withdrawal, and return through the surveyed passage with the full party accounted for. Uttara can finally relinquish her post once the living have taken responsibility and her record is acknowledged.

**Sample:**

> Uttara: “Who is keeping the count?”
>
> Revati: “We are.”

**Result:** the seam is sealed and the order's watch established. The final action commits campaign completion. No new enemy health bar interrupts the emotional resolution.

## 5. Possible character profiles

These are proposed dramatic additions to existing roles. Ages are left open to avoid creating unnecessary chronology conflicts. Names for new characters are working names requiring a consistency pass against `Docs/NAMES_AND_OFFICES.md`.

### Revati — the person who expects you back

- **Public role:** lamp-keeper; checks equipment, tends names, makes the fort feel inhabited.
- **Private want:** to stop preparing people for funerals and start preparing them for careers.
- **Flaw:** withholds information because she thinks uncertainty will keep recruits alive.
- **Contradiction:** remembers everyone but sometimes treats the next recruit as a replacement tool.
- **Relationship progression:** checks your lamp; saves you a seat; uses the wrong predecessor's name; admits what she withheld; joins the relief plan.
- **Ordinary detail:** dislikes ornamental lamps because they are difficult to repair.
- **Voice:** short practical sentences; emotion enters through what she does for someone.
- **Possible line:** “That buckle belonged to Nandan. It held. Keep it.”

### Ganaka — the man who can make a blank official

- **Public role:** tally-keeper.
- **Private want:** to leave a ledger someone else can trust.
- **Flaw:** once used procedure to excuse an omission he knew was wrong.
- **Contradiction:** insists on exact figures but cannot remember a colleague's favourite food.
- **Arc:** records output; accepts one missing name; risks signing a disputed correction; keeps the final watch roll.
- **Ordinary detail:** dries damp pages beside his lunch and complains when they smell of onions.
- **Possible line:** “A blank is permitted. That does not make it true.”

### Visakha — the cost in tomorrow's patients

- **Public role:** physician.
- **Private want:** a week's supply she does not have to bargain for every morning.
- **Flaw:** accepts harmful supply arrangements and calls them temporary long after they become routine.
- **Contradiction:** has little patience for individual promises but remembers every one the player makes.
- **Arc:** asks for a reserve; sees where it comes from; helps plan the transition; reports who still needs treatment after the ending.
- **Ordinary detail:** keeps a plant alive in a chipped measuring cup.
- **Possible line:** “You have brought enough for tonight. I am grateful. Tomorrow remains.”

### Chandrashri — the person who makes a second copy

- **Public role:** governor's clerk.
- **Private want:** another person to sign beside her, rather than praise her courage privately.
- **Flaw:** can value a defensible case more than the witness who must survive giving it.
- **Arc:** has suspicions; gains evidence; learns to protect a witness as carefully as a document; publishes the record.
- **Ordinary detail:** enjoys correcting the governor's exaggerated hunting stories.
- **Possible line:** “Keep the original. I know what happens to originals in this building.”

### Vasumitra — an administrator who chose repeatedly

- **Public role:** governor.
- **Private want:** to hand the problem over while still believing he preserved the province.
- **Flaw:** calls his decisions necessities, then treats other people's alternatives as choices.
- **Human strength:** understands supply, transport, and the cost of a shutdown; can help once he accepts responsibility.
- **Arc:** deflects; is confronted with his authority on the breach; contributes transition logistics without receiving absolution.
- **Possible line:** “The carts will not stop needing guards because I have apologised.”

### Ketu — one of the fifty-seven, new supporting character

- **Role:** yard repairer and rescue survivor; use one existing portrait slot or modest shared presentation initially.
- **Want:** to train somebody else to repair the gate so he can go home before dark.
- **Flaw:** refuses to discuss the rescue when doing so would help; resents being treated as living evidence.
- **Function:** makes Uttara's success tangible, corroborates a tally, supplies rescue knowledge without becoming an exposition dispenser.
- **Possible line:** “She was not gentle. She checked the knot, called me an idiot, and hauled.”
- **Scope:** three major appearances and a small reaction pool; no separate questline.

### The inspector — new active role, name to be selected

- **Want:** to submit a defensible audit, not rescue a province.
- **Flaw:** mistakes the boundaries of his assignment for the boundaries of his responsibility.
- **Strength:** will enforce a documented irregularity even against the governor.
- **Function:** converts the register into access and pressure. Never make him omniscient or correct about everything.
- **Possible line:** “That is outside my instruction. This signature is not.”

### Uttara — a skilled person, still working

- **Want:** everyone accounted for before she leaves.
- **Flaw:** cannot entrust the task to someone who has not demonstrated they will finish it.
- **Human identity:** a demanding manager with habits of inspection and repair, not only a grieving wife or saint.
- **Presentation:** asks practical questions; recognises competent work before recognising a title.
- **Arc:** legend, traces, encounter, trust, relief.
- **Possible line:** “You braced the lower hinge. Good. The upper one lies.”
- **Guardrail:** do not make her mistake the player for her husband. The name signifies a promise; it should not erase the player or reduce her to confusion.

## 6. Succession and optional reactions

The order carries the campaign. A death must not require a new character to pretend they personally witnessed a predecessor's discoveries. Revati briefs successors, Ganaka's copies persist, and dialogue distinguishes “you found” from “the order found.”

Possible recruit introductions:

- “The orchard went to my brother. I kept the debt.”
- “I know the rope work. I would rather not learn the rest.”
- “My daughter thinks I mend the lamps. Let that remain true.”

Possible returning lines:

- After an early bank: “You came back with less than you wanted. You came back.”
- After a death: Revati sets out two cups, then puts one away.
- After recovering a cache: “Was the lamp there?” The reward is not the whole conversation.
- After the house: Ketu notices the clinic lamp staying lit later.
- Before the finale: Ganaka leaves space for everyone going down, including those carrying the tools.

If the player has suffered no deaths, predecessor content can concern an earlier recruit from before their arrival. Never manufacture a player death to unlock the story.

## 7. One ending, consequences worth preparing for

Show five brief epilogue panels using existing portraits and fort views. These are proposed outcomes, not finished canon.

1. **Revati and the watch:** the order keeps a rota at the sealed workings. A name belongs to a person on a shift, not merely a dead predecessor.
2. **Ganaka and Chandrashri:** the corrected roll survives publicly. With corroborating copies, it reaches the capital intact; without them, its local survival still matters and the wider dispute continues.
3. **Visakha:** preparations determine whether the clinic has a reserve or must restrict treatment while replacement supply arrives. Foreshadow this exact cost before closure.
4. **The fifty-seven:** Ketu helps train the new watch. The rescued are people who continue doing things, not a number displayed to prove the ending was good.
5. **Elsewhere:** another province receives the report. Some officials dismiss it; one worker copies the withdrawal plan. Keep the systemic warning while allowing the player's achievement to travel in a small, believable way.

**Proposed final image:** Ganaka's line for the expedition has the same count in both columns.

**Possible last line:** “For once, he did not leave a space.”

This deliberately softens the existing proposal that nobody elsewhere learns anything. The loss remains substantial, but a complete denial of influence would undercut the game's promise of collective persistence.

### Continued play

Do not immediately reopen the same sealed seam as though the finale did not happen. For the first implementation, offer clearly labelled pre-finale free play from a preserved checkpoint. Later, other seams or contracts can support a chronological postgame, but they are additional product scope.

## 8. Gameplay wiring needed

The current foundation includes run results, lifetime rank progress, depth records, read fragments, saved story state, generated rooms, and fort conversations. It does not make the proposed events above automatic.

For each major beat, record: prerequisites, availability, whether it has started, completion, and any outcome. Store campaign facts on persistent order state so death does not erase them. Use stable beat IDs rather than recognising dialogue text.

Suggested facts include `first_name_corrected`, `clinic_reserve`, `gate_repaired`, `house_register_found`, `register_copy_protected`, `audit_opened`, `breach_order_seen`, `seam_surveyed`, `fort_defended`, and `seam_sealed`. Names here are illustrative, not a mandated implementation schema.

Rules to enforce:

- Offer at most one major new scene per return; let players revisit old material freely.
- Use required discoveries to preserve cause and effect. Optional witnesses improve consequences without blocking completion.
- A failed descent can advance reactions and discoveries already earned. Do not require successful banking for every story action.
- Never require the player to spend prana, lose a character, or choose a specific weapon to reach the finale.
- Keep essential shops or substitutes available during the fort crisis; a lost service must not prevent recovery.
- Pause urgent clocks during required text. Disclose stakes before time-sensitive phases.
- Save critical evidence and mission milestones at explicit safe checkpoints. Mission failure retries the unfinished phase rather than erasing hours of story.
- Record campaign completion when the final sealing action succeeds; present epilogue completion separately. This also gives speedruns an unambiguous endpoint.

## 9. What to build first, and what to leave out

### First playable story slice

Build beats 1–4, one corroborated haunting, and the corresponding saved state. This tests whether the player understands the order, likes someone in the fort, and sees why a record matters. Then build the house, whose level and aftermath are the strongest test of the middle act.

### Minimum complete campaign

Implement the causal path from house to audit to breach disclosure to seam to fort defence to relief. Reuse the seam for its final phase. Script the epilogue before expanding reaction pools, so the whole experience can be assessed from beginning to end.

### Defer until the campaign works

- Recurring generated house contracts.
- Multiple endings or restored life-path classes.
- Banked wealth decaying while the player reads or takes a break.
- Large numbers of permanently killable hub characters.
- A fully simulated provincial economy.
- Instability that turns routine hub visits into repeated clearing chores.

The claim that these ideas are “just wiring” should not set the budget. Readable encounters, recovery paths, saves, authored aftermaths, and pacing all need work even when the underlying room and enemy types exist.

## 10. How to judge whether the suggestions improved the game

After the opening, a new player should know one person's name and want to come back. After the house, they should explain the difference between falling production and withheld supply without quoting a lore entry. Before the finale, they should know who opened the door, what Uttara has been doing, and what closing it will cost.

At the ending, they should be able to name something they did that changed somebody's situation. They should understand why the seal can hold, and why it still needs people to keep a promise.

Measure actual first-playthrough time, failed descents, skipped conversations, and the longest stretch without a meaningful change. Adjust encounters and scene placement before raising numerical rank requirements. An 8–10-hour target is successful only if players want the next hour.
