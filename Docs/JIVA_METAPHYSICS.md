# Jiva metaphysics contract

**Locked:** 2026-08-12  
**Authority:** this one-page contract governs every story, dialogue, quest, item description
and mechanic involving jiva stones, black jiva, prana, pretas and the Stambha. It clarifies
the existing plot; it does not change any plot beat, faction or ending.

## The six rules

1. **A jiva is the continuing person, not fuel.** At natural death it ordinarily passes on
   through saṃsāra. The setting is fictional and syncretic: characters and schools disagree
   about what that passage means, but they agree that a person must not be caged after death.

2. **Prana is a renewable imprint left by embodied life.** It is the charge magic spends.
   Prana is not the jiva itself, and no honest speaker may say that a lawful stone “burns a
   soul.” The resource remains morally costly because drawing prana burdens the world and the
   Stambha, even when no person is imprisoned.

3. **A lawful dāna stone is a voluntary release rite.** At natural death, a consenting donor
   permits a prepared stone to receive the pranic imprint released in dying. The jiva is not
   contained: it continues onward once the rite is complete. Consent must be informed,
   revocable while the donor lives and independently witnessed. Ratnapur permits a disclosed
   stipend to the donor or family, but payment purchases the released imprint—not ownership of
   the person. Debt seizure, prison sentences, secret contracts and a family's sale of someone
   else's rite are not dāna. Shantipur disputes whether need permits meaningful consent at all.

4. **A black jiva cages the whole person.** Steya binding tears the jiva from a living or
   unwilling person and traps it in the stone together with its prana. Spending the charge
   damages but does not erase the prisoner. What remains is stranded and may become a
   **preta**. Chapter 01's crime is therefore abduction, exploitation and interrupted passage,
   not merely an unlicensed version of lawful trade.

5. **Animals are never “donors.”** Ordinary beast stones hold pranic residue naturally shed
   through life, migration, moulting, birth or death; drovers collect and sell the charged
   material rather than bind an animal's continuing self. Hunting or slaughter may still be
   ethically disputed, but it is not silently described as consent.

6. **Every use still counts.** The Stambha measures the total prana drawn from the world,
   whether the charge came from dāna, natural residue or steya. It warns when the draw exceeds
   what the world can restore and holds back the accumulated damage. Ending black binding is
   necessary, but it cannot by itself solve the shortage or silence the Stambha.

## What the world currently believes

- Ratnapur law distinguishes **dāna** (a witnessed gift of pranic imprint) from **steya**
  (taking or containing a person). Vikram broke that law and invokes āpad-dharma to defend it.
- Shantipur argues that poverty makes even formal consent coercive and that all prana demand
  eventually produces steya. This remains a serious challenge, not a misunderstanding.
- Marukot argues that ending prana use immediately would kill people who need powered water,
  food storage and defence. Its emergency is real.
- The Siddha Order believes efficiency and new workings can outrun scarcity. It has measured
  output while failing to measure the world's burden.
- The Sabha regulates conduct without knowing the Stambha's true function. The Dhruva Order
  knows enough to fear disclosure but not enough to repair the instrument.

## Writer and implementation guardrails

- Say **charge a stone**, **draw/spend prana**, **release rite**, or **bind a jiva**. Use
  **burn a soul** only as an accusation against black binding, never as the lawful mechanism.
- A lawful stone may be tragic, commercialised or socially coercive, but it does not imprison
  a jiva. A black jiva always does.
- Pretas are victims. Combat should read as release, pacification or severing a binding—not
  punishment of an evil species.
- Chapter 01 may show the pulse and competing explanations, but must not reveal that the
  Stambha measures draw, warns and holds. That remains the Chapter 06 reveal.
- These terms draw inspiration from multiple living Indian traditions but do not assert that
  those traditions share one metaphysics. In-world disagreement must remain visible, and
  culturally sensitive final copy requires human review.
