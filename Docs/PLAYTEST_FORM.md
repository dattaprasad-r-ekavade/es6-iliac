# The playtest form

A template to be recreated in Google Forms and linked from the itch.io page.

**Design rules it follows, because they decide the response rate:**

- **Under three minutes.** Every extra question costs responses, and a half-finished form
  tells you nothing. Twelve questions, most of them one tap.
- **No demographics.** Age, country and hours-played buy nothing at a sample size of five.
- **Nothing is required except the one question.** A person who answers only that is still a
  useful response; a form that refuses to submit is a lost one.
- **The important question is first**, while attention is highest. Standard practice is to put
  the open box last, and standard practice is wrong here — this game has exactly one thing it
  needs to know.
- **It asks what happened, not what they thought.** *"Did you hesitate"* is recallable.
  *"Was the tension well-paced"* is a request for a review.

**Settings to use:** collect email addresses **off**, limit to one response **off** (both need a
Google account and cost responses), progress bar **on**, "Send responses to a spreadsheet" **on**.

## Title

**Ratna Bay — alpha feedback**

## Description

> Thank you for playing. This is an early alpha built by one person, and there is one thing I
> genuinely cannot find out on my own.
>
> Two minutes, nothing required, no account needed. Answer only the first question if that is all
> you have time for — it is the one that matters.

---

## 1. At the shut door, did you ever actually hesitate? *(Required)*

*Multiple choice*

> After clearing a room, the game asks whether to bank what you have or press on. I need to know
> whether that was a decision or a formality.

- Yes — I genuinely had to think about it, more than once
- Once or twice, early on, then I settled into a habit
- No — I always did the same thing without thinking
- I did not get far enough to meet it

## 2. If you did hesitate — what were you weighing?

*Paragraph, optional*

> Anything at all. What you had, what you were afraid of, what you were hoping for.

## 3. How many runs did you play?

*Multiple choice*

- 1
- 2
- 3
- 4 to 6
- More than 6

## 4. What made you stop playing?

*Multiple choice, with "Other"*

> The most useful question on the form after the first one. Please be blunt.

- I had had enough and was satisfied
- I got bored
- I got frustrated or stuck
- Something broke
- I ran out of time, but I would have kept going
- I could not get it to run at all

## 5. Where were you when you stopped?

*Short answer, optional*

> A room number if you remember one, or "the yard", "the shop", "the fort".

## 6. Did anything confuse you?

*Paragraph, optional*

> Somewhere you did not know what to do, a screen you could not read, a word that meant nothing
> to you, or a key that did not do what you expected.

## 7. Did the fighting feel good?

*Linear scale, 1 to 5* — "Floaty and weightless" → "Solid, I felt the hits"

## 8. How did it sound?

*Linear scale, 1 to 5* — "Bad or annoying" → "Good"

> The effects are generated by code rather than recorded, and I am trying to find out whether
> that is audible to anybody who was not told.

## 9. Did the game look like a place?

*Linear scale, 1 to 5* — "Programmer art" → "Somewhere I believed in"

> All of the art is generated in code, including the faces. Honest answers help more than kind
> ones.

## 10. Did anything break, or look wrong?

*Paragraph, optional*

> Anything that floated, flickered, went through a wall, or should not have been there.

## 11. Would you play it again when there is more of it?

*Multiple choice*

- Yes
- Maybe
- No

## 12. Anything else at all?

*Paragraph, optional*

## Closing message

> That is everything — thank you. Genuinely: five people answering this is the difference between
> guessing and knowing.
>
> If you would rather say something longer, or you hit a bug you can describe, the itch.io page
> has a comments section and an email address.

## The three answers to read first

When the responses land, these are the ones the whole exercise exists for. Everything else is
supporting detail.

1. **Q1** — is the camp decision real for anybody who is not its author? Nine owner sessions
   say yes at a median 2.1s across 28 doors. That number is currently one person's.
2. **Q3** — did anybody reach three runs? That is the production plan's own gate for iteration
   14, still unmet.
3. **Q4** — *why* they stopped. A game that people quit while still enjoying has a different
   problem from one people quit out of boredom, and the fixes have nothing in common.
