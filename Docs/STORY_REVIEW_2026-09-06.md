# Ratna Bay — review of the proposed script

This is feedback on `SCRIPT.md`, not a replacement canon or an implementation of its proposed acts. The older `COMPLETE_OUTLINE.md` and `STORY.md` do not yet describe the same ending structure. Review date: 6 September 2026.

## Verdict

The new arc is stronger than the earlier outline. Uttara gives the economics a human centre. The movement from a comforting story about a rescuer to the person still holding her post is the best part of the plot. The fifty-seven survivors give her a life defined by competence, rather than only by sacrifice. Keep the miners' naming of the order: "You are the one somebody is coming for" makes succession emotionally meaningful.

Act II also has a good shape now: trader, physician, armourer, wealthy house, inspection, then the order itself. Each stage can change a familiar place and relationship. The house is particularly valuable because it turns an abstract shortage into an encounter and a physical document. Avoid burying that discovery in a long explanatory conversation.

The weak points are causal clarity, conflicting chronology, and the risk that all endings feel like the author punishing the player for participating. These are fixable without replacing the premise.

## Improve each act through one playable discovery

### Act I: the comfort has evidence

Let the player meet one of the fifty-seven early. Give that survivor a practical life and an ordinary annoyance, not a memorial speech. A worker fixing a leaking roof while complaining about the price of lamp fuel establishes both the value and cost of the economy.

A good false detail in the legend: Uttara carried a golden lamp. At the bottom, the player finds an ordinary repaired brass lamp with the same distinctive bent handle. The legend exaggerated the object, not the rescue. Avoid a twist that reveals the admired woman to be secretly cruel; it would discard what makes her compelling.

Make the first accounting anomaly physical. A tally-stick has marks for more people than the wage roll names. Let the player ask about it. "Nobody explains anything, even if the world stays confusing for an hour" conflicts with this game's intended accessibility. Keep optional practical questions: a novice hire has every reason to ask how the work is paid and who keeps the roll.

### Act II: give each conversation an action and a consequence

The physician should force a modest allocation decision involving somebody already known. The army's priority should appear as supplies physically leaving while an order request is refused. The house should first feel welcoming, comfortable and offensively ordinary; the register in the cellar then changes how its warmth reads.

Do not treat taking loose stones as self-evidently wrong. Taking a hoard to pay for medical care and taking it for personal accumulation are different actions. If the ledger remembers, remember the destination or the explicit promise made, not only the pickup count.

Make the first fort disruption local and recoverable: a room closed, a service moved, an occupant displaced. Save the full timed overrun for the finale. Two similar full fort disasters would blunt each other.

Keep the inspector difficult to persuade, but give the player useful things to do with him. He can refuse a moral appeal while still being bound to accept admissible evidence, a witnessed correction or an official copy. A procedural antagonist works best when the procedure is playable. "He is right about everything" would make him the author's voice; being rigorous about one narrow mandate is more convincing.

### Act III: meeting Uttara changes the objective

An encounter can start as a fight because she protects the threshold, but attacking should not be the only language available in its resolution. Show what she is defending. A lamp, a recovered name or the last tally can change the encounter once the player has survived long enough to understand it.

Distinguish remembering Uttara from formally acknowledging her death. People can love a legend while an administrative category continues to erase the actual woman. Establish why she was left "missing" or recorded only as an office; otherwise a famous superintendent having no written name anywhere strains belief.

The timed fort finale must not rush essential reading. Pause combat while a conversation is open, or give rescued characters a safe gathering place to speak. Signal which rescues are time-sensitive before the clock begins. A first-roguelite audience should not lose somebody because a necessary paragraph took thirty seconds to read.

## Make the three endings follow demonstrated rules

**Establish what a true count does.** Earlier, let Ganaka correct one dead worker's name with evidence and a witness. On returning underground, that particular haunting is gone. This demonstrates that acknowledgement matters without letting a player solve an empire by typing arbitrary names into a book. Decide whether the requirement is truth, public recognition, restitution, or a ritual act; it must be more than a magical spelling test.

**The Ledger needs an economic cause for its cost.** If accurate records release the dead, why can the mines not simply reopen safely? A workable answer is that the truthful accounts expose the unlawful extraction on which the energy budget depends; lawful supply is smaller. The inspector's shutdown or the loss of coerced supply has to be established before the choice. Do not attach "the town ends" as an unexplained fee for doing the requested moral act.

**The Lamp needs bounded differences.** Rationing, limits on extraction and priority for medical need are meaningfully different from efficient exploitation with no limits. Let earlier choices determine which safeguards the order can actually enforce, and show both the lives saved and the continued harm. The script says it never grades, then calls the player a villain: remove that verdict and let the consequences speak.

**The Release needs an answer to reopening.** The province already broke the seal once. What makes the player's action more durable? Perhaps the order agrees to guard the threshold and changes its employment, rather than one disposable successor remaining alone forever. If recurrence is intentional, acknowledge it honestly; do not present a temporary seal as a permanent solution.

The default Lamp outcome should be clearly telegraphed. A skipped conversation or missed rank gate must not silently select an ending. Show an explicit final decision, even if maintaining the arrangement is the initially highlighted choice.

Keep the epilogues about named people. Foreshadow the major costs; surprise the player with particular human consequences, not a previously undisclosed rule that invalidates the decision. Give preparation some effect: supplies secured, records copied, a physician warned, survivors organised. This can be a handful of epilogue conditions rather than three branching campaigns.

## Small additions with high value

- Revati starts to say a predecessor's name, catches herself, and uses the successor's. One line makes continuity uncomfortable and personal.
- Ganaka leaves a blank line on the roll for the missing until the player can supply a name. The empty space becomes a recurring visual motif.
- The rescued survivor describes Uttara as a demanding manager who checked knots and counted twice. Her skill matters as much as her courage.
- Let one mine testimony contradict a seemingly reliable official document. The Ledger route then needs corroboration, not automatic trust in either a ghost or a clerk.
- Before the final choice, show all the people who now depend on the order in one already-built room. The abstract options acquire faces without a new region.

Do not add recurring generated house contracts yet. The first house already earns its production cost through the story; a second repeatable mode is a separate product decision.

## Suggested next writing milestone

Author and playtest the first six descents, three survivor/Revati reactions, one small accounting correction, and the house discovery. This tests the warm opening, the rules of release, and the central political reveal before committing to hundreds of fragments or the finale.
