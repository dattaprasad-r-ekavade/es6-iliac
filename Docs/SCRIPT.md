# Ratna Bay — The Script

**Status:** proposed, and the place to argue before anything is written into content files.
[`STORY.md`](STORY.md) is the structure. [`STORY_AND_SYSTEMS.md`](STORY_AND_SYSTEMS.md) is where
it meets the mechanics. **This is the plot** — what is true, who wants what, and what is
actually said.

---

## 1. The keystone: what the first Deepankar found

Everything hangs off one fact, and until now it was a blank. `gov.2` says she went down to close
the seam and something stopped her. Nothing stopped her.

> **She closed it. It worked. Ratna Bay starved.**
>
> The lamps went out, the foundries stopped, the physicians had nothing, and in one season the
> province lost more people than the mines had taken in twenty years. So the order went back
> down and **opened it again** — and then rewrote what it was for.

Every Deepankar since has been maintaining the thing the order was founded to end. **They know.**
It is why the hall is evasive, why the shrine is uncomfortable, why nobody says the old name out
loud. The order is not corrupt. It is ashamed, and it is right to be, and it is not obvious that
it was wrong.

This does four things no other version of the answer does:

- It makes the governor's line **literally true** rather than an excuse: *"the alternative is a
  province that starves in a season."* He is not lying. He has the precedent.
- It explains **capture without a villain.** Nobody had to be bought. They saw what closing it
  cost and could not do it twice.
- It gives **The Release** a real price. The player would be repeating a documented catastrophe
  on purpose, with the numbers in front of them.
- It puts the moral argument **in the past tense.** The question is not *should someone do the
  right thing* — someone did, and this is what happened. That is a far harder question and a far
  better one.

### And she is still down there

Not as a body. **Uttara never rose and never left.** Whatever holds the seam from becoming a
door is her, still doing the job, three hundred years past the point of it meaning anything.

She is the fight at the bottom, and she is not an enemy. She is the last Deepankar still working.

## 2. Why the supply is failing *now*

The middle act needs a cause or it is just errands. The cause is political, not geological, and
it is the setting's own:

> **The capital raised the quota.**

Mines fill the treasury; the treasury pays the army. An empire preparing for something wants
more prana, and the province cannot meet it from the lawful supply — the almost-dead do not die
faster to order. So the difference comes out of the living, and it has been growing for nine
years, which is exactly what `clerk.1` already says.

**And this time the inspection arrives.** `barracks.2` already establishes that a mahamatra
comes every five years and *"the last one did not reach us."* This one does.

That gives the middle act a **face, a deadline, and an escalation** out of a line that is
already written.

---

## 3. Who the protagonist is

**The order.** Not the player character — the player character dies, repeatedly, by design.

This has to be a conscious rule or the writing will fight the succession mechanic. Individual
Deepankars get a **voice** — one line about who they were, a manner, a way of talking to the
tally-keeper. The **arc** belongs to the Dipadhara: an order that was founded to end this, failed
by succeeding, has spent three centuries maintaining it, and is handed one more chance by
somebody who keeps coming back up.

Practically: nobody in the fort ever says *"you"* about the founding. They say *"we."*

---

## 4. What the preta want

They are beings of unsatisfiable want. It is worth deciding precisely what they want, because it
turns out to be the plot.

> **To be counted.**

*"Forty of us went in on the governor's order. The register says twelve."*

The mountain is full of people the state did not write down. That is why they cannot rest and
why prana holds them: they are owed a number. It also makes the humblest room in the fort — the
gate, the tally-keeper, a table and a ledger — the room the ending turns on.

**And Uttara is uncounted too.** The register lists the office, not the name. The founder of the
order suffered the same erasure as the convict gangs she went down to answer for, and nobody has
ever noticed because nobody reads the old rolls.

## 5. The cast, and where the pressure comes from

There is **no villain**, and that should be defended rather than fixed. There is a quota, and
everybody in the fort is somewhere between complicit and trapped.

| Who | Wants | Is trapped by |
|---|---|---|
| **Tissa**, tally-keeper | The books to balance | Not being permitted to say they do not |
| **Sona**, lamp-keeper | Somebody to know the old name | Three centuries of the order's silence |
| **Visakha**, physician | To stop buying what she buys | People who will die without it |
| **Suvarnapala**, registrar | The quota met | The capital, who set it |
| **Isidata**, priest | The dead pitied | Being tolerated only while he stays quiet |
| **Chandrashri**, clerk | The true figures read | Three replies telling her to check her arithmetic |
| **Vasumitra**, governor | To hand it to somebody else | Thirty years of choosing none of the options |
| **The mahamatra** | The province audited | Nothing. He is the only free person in it |

**The inspector is the antagonist and is not evil.** He is doing his job correctly. That is what
makes him frightening: there is no argument that works on him, because he is not wrong about
anything he is actually claiming.

## 6. The beat sheet

### Act I — The job (runs 1–8, ranks Yukta to Sthanika)

The economy, learned by doing. Clear caves, get paid, rise a little. The fort is warm and the
work is honest, and the only strange thing is that the ledger never balances and the tally-keeper
will not discuss it.

**Beats already written:** `gate.1`, `hall.1`, `assay.1`, `forge.1`.

**New, and needed:** the first line of a preta's testimony, in a mine, on a kill. This is the
first crack and it should arrive before any fort room admits anything.

> **Turn I** — *the supply does not add up.* `phys.1`. The physician buys prana and will not say
> from whom. The order sends you to find out why the price is rising.

### Act II — The complicity (runs 8–25, Pradeshika to Adhyaksha)

You are the supply chain now. The errand chain from `STORY_AND_SYSTEMS.md` §4 runs here, and
every step is a conversation with a decision:

1. **The trader.** Argue the price down. What you offer costs the order something.
2. **The shortage.** Explain it — to the registrar, or the physician. What you say is recorded.
3. **The armourer.** The order wants supplies the province will not release.
4. **The inspection is announced.** The mahamatra is coming. The books do not balance.
5. **The fort falls.** Rooms are held. This is instability arriving as plot rather than weather.
6. **The questions.** You start asking the clerk, then the governor, things nobody asks.

> **Turn II** — *the first Deepankar.* `gov.2`. Her name, and that she went down to close it.
> The governor does not tell you she succeeded. He does not know.

### Act III — The seam, and the choice

**The seam.** One authored descent. You find Uttara, learn that it worked, and learn what it
cost. This is the only hand-authored level in the game and this is why it earns it.

**The fort falls properly.** What happens at the seam destabilises everything. The finale is the
fort overrun, cleared room by room against a clock — and each room you reach is one more person
telling you what they would do.

**The choice**, made with all three prices known.

## 7. The three endings, with their actual costs

| | What you do | What happens | Who pays |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The Ledger** | Write the true count. Every name, including Uttara's | The dead are counted and rest. The seam quiets because nothing is left wanting. The capital reads the corrected books and shuts the province | The living. Ratna Bay is finished as a place |
| **The Lamp** | Take the trade over and run it properly | The province lives, meets the quota, and prospers. The drip-feed becomes efficient and permanent | Whoever is being drained. You become the thing the order was founded to stop |
| **The Release** | Close the seam, as she did | It works, exactly as it worked before | Everyone, in one season, with the precedent in front of you |

**None is clean, and the game never says which is right.** The Ledger is the one the preta are
asking for and it starves the town. The Lamp is the one that saves the most lives and makes you
the villain of the next three hundred years. The Release is the honest one and it is a
catastrophe somebody already ran the experiment on.

**Default: The Lamp.** A player who committed to nothing gets the ending for people who commit
to nothing, and the province quietly goes on as it was. That is not a punishment. It is the
truest outcome of indifference and it should be delivered without comment.

## 8. Rules for the writing itself

Precision here is worth more than volume, so these are constraints rather than suggestions.

**Nobody explains the setting.** No character has ever had to be told what a jiva stone is. If a
line only exists to inform the player, cut it and let the world be confusing for another hour.

**Two registers, held consistently.** Officials speak in Sanskrit forms and bureaucratic nouns;
everybody else uses Prakrit forms and short sentences. An official who slips into the common
register is being kind, and a local who uses the formal one is being sarcastic. That is
characterisation for free and it costs nothing to maintain.

**Nobody argues the theme.** No speeches about greed. The verse on the pillar is the only place
the argument is stated outright, and it was carved by the state, in a mine the state opened, to
take wealth out of it. That irony does the work; adding a character who notices it out loud
ruins it.

**One idea per fragment, two lines at most.** They are read between runs by somebody with a door
waiting. A fragment that needs a paragraph is two fragments, or it is a scene.

**The order says "we" about the founding.** Always. It is three hundred years old and the shame
is inherited.

**Preta testimony is always a number and a name, never a plea.** *"Forty went in. The register
says twelve."* Not *"help us."* The horror is the arithmetic.

## 9. Still open

- **Uttara's name.** Chosen for sound and because it is attested, but the order should have a
  second name for her that the register does not — which is the erasure, in one detail.
- **What the empire is arming for.** The quota rose for a reason. It probably never needs to be
  said, and the fact that nobody in the province knows is better than any answer.
- **Whether the mahamatra can be turned.** Currently no, and that is stronger. Worth one pass to
  be sure the game is not simply refusing the player something they will reasonably expect.
- **Does the player ever learn the order reopened the seam?** It is the darkest fact in the game.
  Suggested: it is in the hall, from Sona, at the highest rank — the lamp-keeper is the only one
  who would say it, and only to somebody who has earned it.
- **Names for the province and the governor's family**, still unfixed from `SETTING.md`.
